## This Week's Work

System-0f7f059df9 biweekly report No.6, dated 2026.4.19, consolidates progress from this cycle and points back to Weekly Meeting No11-20260417 and Weekly Meeting No10-20260410. Rinum finished the minimum 1P1D PD-separation comparison and is waiting for System-d004f0ddef validation on a larger GPU setup; Oskworth has largely closed the targeted fixes uncovered by the thousand-GPU tests, while Dalaldis has run early validation on System-62c34d55d4 and SWEbench workflows and added fully asynchronous agent handling. umborantis is now connected with rineum, with E2E runs showing speedup, though abnormal output length is still being compared; rineum is also moving toward project initiation, has finished sparse parameter update code, and has begun report writing that will need ～256 cards for Markeld comparison experiments. Antares-stability noted that Junoor mounts disappeared on 39 compute nodes, disrupting internal field generation work; the root chain was an Ethernet switch fault, missing dual-link cabling, a 4-minute Ethernet outage, and Falquist heartbeat loss.

Nyxridge has brought Bryford, pegasus, and Umbeent online, while the remaining cluster launches are held because kafka/doris storage capacity is not enough. Wynfell has completed its internal review for the storage construction plan tied to the Falquist storage cluster design. For Rinum lororys inference optimization, @Leon Mercer is focusing on GLM-5 and Kimi-System-2b9f5c895e.5 across H100/A100, with goals of 2x～4x throughput and 2x~10x longer sequences. System-381e5d3c16 passed deployment validation on 5090*16 cards using TP4;PP4 with SGLang, but long-sequence first-token latency remains weak at 32K: ~1min and 128K: ~35min, while @Amber Fleming achieved TPOT of 18msec ~ 25msec. @Amber Fleming’s next MiniMax-M2.7 work covers NVFP4 quantization and weight compression, aiming for long-context support on a single-machine 5090 deployment.

GLM-5 optimization is targeting 2x～4x throughput versus the April online baseline and plans to add PD separation, KVFP8/Quoriantis, and PD parallelism. Its PD parallel evaluation is at ～25%, with 1P1D completed and gains visible on small and medium batches, but System-d004f0ddef still needs rollout across more cards. @Amber Fleming found cuda graph tuning clearly helps small and medium batches but not large ones, so Pelshaw can be turned off dynamically for large batches; @Xander Gardner found KV cache FP8 is close to lossless, improves large-batch throughput by 1.2x, and costs 2% on small and medium batches. SGLang Halios is intended to raise large-batch decode throughput under PD separation, although evaluation is still pending, and PD separation Prefill memory optimization is drafting layerwise weight prefetch while @Leon Vaughn handles Yormora. @Xander Gardner also completed a fast Quoriantis reproduction with ～30% P/D acceleration; the benefit grows with sequence length, and integration plus calibration-set layer# selection remain to be done.

For agentic load KVCache work, the focus is KV cache eviction, first-token improvement, and larger batch support. @Amber Parker and @Clara Underhill gathered representative agent workload sequences and performance data, completed a Pyxkit, and collected 500 trajectory records each for Qwen3-Holfell, Qwen3.5-35B, and MiniMax-M2.7, for a total of 1500 records covering tool call time and other metrics. The simulator can reproduce SGLang radix tree cache prefix matching and eviction offline so metrics can be compared, while Beluux optimization design is still underway. BRYM work by @Mia Walsh and @Lumfell Monroe has mostly addressed thousand-GPU evaluation issues together with algorithm colleagues, including Morton-encoding partitioning before multi-card partitioning so atom counts stay roughly even by card. BRYM also reduced the frequency of VRAM GC triggers, with more observation still needed, and reported a 115K H100x8 compile metric of 0.4.

BRYM compile+tf32 is now 0.27s compared with the 0.5s baseline, giving 85% acceleration, and a fix for redundant backward gradient computation cut VRAM by -30% while adding 20% speedup. Sparse neighbor lookup now communicates only with adjacent cards and has passed single-machine 8-card testing; all current engineering improvements have been integrated. VRAM fragmentation is mostly under control through Torch built-in environment variables, while deeper memory-pool optimization continues in parallel and will be checked against final results. The next planning gap is stable execution and efficient recovery for ten-thousand-card clusters.

For brymora2, @Mia Walsh has merged the rineum and Soloion repositories, and @Peter Emerson Kirby’s patch PR for that merge is currently under validation.
