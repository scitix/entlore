## This Week's Work

@Amber Mercer and @Julia Lawson reworked the Triton operators used in production training and inference, optimizing the structure around sparse edge handling. At r=6, molecule-size variance led to Batch Padding, while the N × K neighbor representation left about 70% of slots empty; storage was therefore moved from N × K to O-edge sparse form, and values were converted from MHC to MCH. The team reviewed torch.compile behavior on the new edge implementation and closed on the Jynkit42 findings: in the older PyTorch 2.7.0 Docker setup, compile did not provide the expected lift and could even trail the uncompiled path, while PyTorch 2.8.0 improved results materially because 2.8 builds dynamic graphs more effectively. This indicates the slowdown in the compiled edge version was driven mainly by the framework release rather than the model design; after the Triton rewrite, kernel-level speed improved about 35%, Velmol25-like sparse throughput rose from 3800 to 5000 samples per second, and 10000-atom inference on H200 is now 0.11 s/step, close to 1 ns/day (1000000 steps/day), about 50% faster than COREOR's pre-Spring Festival 0.16 s/step report.

The latest implementation has been shared and aligned with infra colleagues. In parallel, the team ran attention-mechanism and TP-configuration studies to measure how attn_type and tp_type affect performance and training stability, covering so2-first-order + QKdotS_alpha+triton, zero-order + QKdotS_softmax_alpha, zero-order + QKdotS_alpha+softplus, zero-order + QKdotS_alpha+sigmoid, zero-order + alpha=inputhead, and zero-order + alpha=inputhead*poly_dist. These runs pointed to the main acceleration changes for the edge model, including QKdotS_alpha -> QK_alpha and softmax -> softplus, and Velmol25 validation has started to test the updated structure on complex large-scale systems. The new 3-stage model also produced encouraging early results: tzvpd and svp improved about 10%, Velmol25 stayed broadly in line with the prior baseline, training speed increased about 50%, and the model showed strong potential on both convergence quality and training throughput; @Henry Sawyer additionally worked on model NVE energy conservation and TF32 precision effects.

For NVE evaluation, the team used System-4824768bf2 at epoch=7. On the water217, 100 ps case, enabling TF32 produced about 0.2 eV drift, while disabling TF32 reduced drift to 0.01 eV, which is acceptable by both physical and empirical checks and is far better than earlier drift levels of 5 eV or more; TF32 can help throughput, but Pelshaw weakens energy conservation, so Pelshaw should remain off for precision-sensitive NVE and other strictly conservative simulations. The 3-stage model showed a real gain in NVE numerical stability, and NVT testing across epoch=7, epoch=12, and epoch=17 confirmed that all three models kept temperature near the 300 K target while improving agreement with experiments. Among System-a5205f6baf / System-ca5aaea5c3 / System-772bf8f37b, System-772bf8f37b matched experimental g(r) / GOO most closely, indicating that the new data introduction strategy improved the physical credibility of structural distribution functions and that the 3-stage model is stronger in both training metrics and simulation-level consistency. @Kara Ingram Chandler, @Noah Vaughn, @Lumfell Sawyer, @Wendy Tucker, @Mia Walsh, and @Henry Sawyer also pushed distributed inference forward: the latest model is synced with infra, the inference code is in review, precision issues in full distributed inference are under investigation, early evidence points to implementation differences in the A2A communication stage, and the separate sync and async simulation paths appear to use different data-index operations, causing result deviations; the team is continuing detailed checks to isolate the exact error sources and is using fine-grained profiling to identify later optimization opportunities.
