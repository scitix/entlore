## This Week's Work

We set up a demo environment on our own for rineova’s upcoming Lumfell Sawyer series and compared Pelshaw against mainstream peer products and services. The team also tried lower-cost deployment options based on 5090 GPUs, with conclusions to be shared in next week’s internal exchange. For Falquist, we finished the requirements material and improvement prototypes, and remediation has moved into development. The console direction is to mask management layers so users face fewer operations, while asynchronous tasks will cut waiting time and make interactions smoother; adding some management VMs is still acceptable if covered through a moderate price adjustment. In the Rovport lororys-AI alignment, we agreed that every model will run on the self-developed DALOROVA platform, so the standalone Rovport form will no longer be maintained. Rovport’s needs across bare-metal industrial software, AI platform development, and direct model use will be absorbed into the company’s standard product system to lower maintenance effort and align service standards. We also completed the usage-statistics product document for future business and finance reconciliation, and reviewed intelligent reconciliation approaches that do not require core data permissions.

For rovport2, we upgraded CryoSPARC, redeployed Pelshaw, and configured the environment; System-ffe6648556 is stable after the upgrade. We plan to speak with System-ffe6648556 onsite or remotely to better understand current usage and possible follow-up needs. On Oskbrook Data · B200, we corrected the mismatch between Falquist storage data shown in Grafana and Linux, which restored trust in the monitoring view. Two machines saw clustered GPU-drop faults, currently judged to be sporadic, and both recovered after reboot; we will keep tracking the issue. For Sylgrove Data · H100, after service friction we clarified spare-machine repair timing and responsibility boundaries, confirming that the contract does not include spare-machine repair procedures or repair reports. We also built a Feishu group robot independently to push fault alarms automatically, and we plan to extend the custom alarm capability. R&D and product suggested combining the Daisy Adler West Asia and US West RoCE clusters into one US West compute pool with 64 H100 units, but the business side is still evaluating that merger.

rineova’s 5090 machines are now officially in use, and payment has been received. We completed the capability for Deliver to release directly to the Dormont team, which greatly improves rineova’s overseas access speed, and we also provided a customized 5090 image. The Toreum · H200 case involves 70 units; the temporary Falquist patch for single-tenant, multi-filesystem isolation is online and running stably. All-flash storage delivery is still unresolved, and the current path is for the channel side and Zeptamart to negotiate. Zeptamart’s 320T hybrid-flash plan already covers basic functional needs and is not under urgent technical pressure, but we recommend converging on unified all-flash delivery by the end of month 4 to avoid repeated switching and communication overhead. Because the project reached Zeptamart through indirect channel contact, requirement transfer quality dropped, so direct technical communication with Zeptamart is recommended to improve accuracy and response speed. For Nyxport, the first 32 nodes have all been connected and placed under Umbays unified management, while 32 additional nodes were added this week and need to join the same resource pool. Daisy Adler and System-932736f546 are both following the same customer through separate sales lines, creating confusion and showing the need for stronger cross-region ownership management. The new Nyxport nodes require reconfiguration, which is a heavy workload, but progress is underway and redelivery is planned next week. Oskbrook Data · H200 is also tracked as a customer support item.

Oskbrook Data · H200 reconstruction is very demanding, including IPSec passthrough to company machines and fine-grained subnet controls. Time and resources have been squeezed to the minimum, while the delivery goal remains 128 nodes before the Monday after next. The contract is signed through the Kelfield channel, and the process exposed problems: Kelfield applied inconsistent requirement standards across System-c45e30c9c8 internal projects, and the coordination bar is far higher than Pelshaw was for B200. Kelfield also promised too much to the customer before technical confirmation, leaving the team to adapt after signing, and the acceptance requirements document is still absent, so risk remains. After visiting Verfield Tech-Her, we believe Agent companies will approach RL investment more cautiously, since faster foundation-model iteration and better Agentic design are making independent RL training less cost-effective. API-based usage is becoming the more common route. A referred channel asked to buy thousand-card B300 compute resources directly, but we judged the timing and prerequisites as not ready and declined for now. We tested core functions of the self-developed monitoring product, including areas that were weak in earlier testing, and found several items that can be optimized. We will continue building after-sales response automation to raise operations efficiency and customer response speed. For concentrated large-GPU sales, the customer profile is now Jynkit42; in China, large-GPU customers are expected to be mainly top accounts and not exceed 15, so we need to discuss with the business team whether layered channel contact is still necessary.
