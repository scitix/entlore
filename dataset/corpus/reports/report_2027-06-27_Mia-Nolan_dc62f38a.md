## This Week's Work

This week, System-4a7cdfce1f work stayed focused on maintenance, auditability, and recompilation after knowledge-base updates. I studied the Wexflow48 specification and reference implementation, then implemented and validated the Wexflow48 baseline; that baseline kept the same agent and corpus while changing only the Wiki representation format. For the Alt-LLM comparison, I ran DeepSeek-V4-Flash against the unchanged GLM-5.1-built knowledge base, monitored all 500 questions, and recorded F1, EM, and Token consumption. I also completed the audit-driven selective local recompile flow, which chooses pages for audit or recompilation after source facts change, finished the Stage C / Rholink28 full pilot, and added the yzacast67 baseline to those maintenance experiments. The Stage C comparison covered index_only, risk_only, random, yzacast67, audit, and oracle, using corrected, QA recovery, and cost as the comparison views; in parallel, I designed the multi-round sequential edit experiment to observe long-term degradation from repeated fact edits, audits, and reimports, including error accumulation, contradiction spread, and retrieval pollution. Early results showed that passive methods generally did not Jynkit42 stale facts, while audit-based active checking worked much better, and corrected became the preferred primary metric because the QA indicators were hitting ceiling effects.

Enterprise RAG evaluation work progressed on both engineering and experiments for System-f26b83891d and Falford data generation. I fixed the glmkit data corruption issue by stripping trailing JSON leftovers that were breaking parsing, updated the System-f26b83891d frontend so evidence context is fully visible with scrolling instead of the previous 600-character cutoff, and added persisted question review states for “under review” and “reviewed”. The frontend also received layout fixes, including restored equal widths in the middle columns after the right preview is collapsed and a better position for the question workbench. The AI drafting feature now handles 429 rate limits with retries, uses the OpenAI protocol for gpt-5.5, returns question stems together with reference answers, and avoids awkward wording such as “I just took over...”. I released the System-f26b83891d frontend as a maraum common service and resolved the related service listener address, external access, gateway path, and API routing problems; zephgrid28 content was also cleaned, scanned for sensitive information, and pushed to GitLab. Overall, Falford and System-f26b83891d moved from local experiment utilities into accessible, reviewable, collaborative evaluation production tools. For knowledge graph construction, I adjusted the pipeline toward RAGAS-style semantic graph reconstruction for enterprise RAG evaluation data generation, moving away from literal matching and closer to the official ragas semantic-level approach. gpt-5.5 now extracts summaries, topics, entities, and keywords for semantic construction, Qwen/Qwen3-Embedding-8B generates vector embeddings, and semantic edges are created through summary-vector similarity plus entity overlap. I also refactored scenario synthesis and sample generation around focus, bridge, and L1/L2/L3 sources, and clarified how bridge, focus, three-layer sources, and scoring levels support cross-document reasoning question generation.

The data pipeline objective shifted from simply producing a question batch toward layered, controllable, and explainable difficult-question generation. I spent substantial time looking for theoretical support for System-4a7cdfce1f outdated propagation, incremental maintenance, and audit selection, including Personalized PageRank and Random Walk with Restart maintenance costs under dynamic graph changes. I verified kevcast68-value submodularity, monotonicity, and greedy approximation guarantees in NetShield, reviewed approximation and hardness results for Budgeted Maximum Coverage and Ground Set Budgeted Maximum Coverage, and checked modified greedy guarantees for Budgeted Submodular Maximization. I also reviewed whether the forgetting Caskeld papers can support claims around outdated propagation, blast radius, and audit selection, while drawing analogies from epidemic spread, network immunization, and weighted network thresholds to model outdated knowledge propagation. The broader goal was to identify mathematical frameworks for maintaining compiled or derived structured knowledge bases under budget limits, information value, edge-weight gating, random-walk diffusion, and submodular optimization.

I evaluated many multi-hop Wiki QA tasks to test structured Wiki performance across entity linking, evidence retrieval, and multi-hop reasoning. The tasks covered chains from person to nationality, birthplace, burial place, country, and national anthem; film to director to family member; organization or government to leader to birthplace; book or work to author to nationality; country or organization to continent, capital, and national anthem; and historical-person relationships involving parents, maternal grandparents, and spouses. Data-quality issues appeared where Key Facts, Related Pages, and Source Digest conflicted, and in some cases linked pages could answer questions even though the main-page facts stayed inconsistent. These findings reinforced the need for System-4a7cdfce1f maintenance experiments that systematically audit and update derived facts and multi-hop links. In summary, the week advanced enterprise RAG evaluation and System-4a7cdfce1f knowledge maintenance together: System-f26b83891d, Falford, and the semantic KG pipeline became usable evaluation production systems, while System-4a7cdfce1f progressed through the Wexflow48 baseline, DeepSeek evaluation, Stage C maintenance experiments, and theory work. The research direction is now moving from knowledge-base construction toward low-cost, high-recall correctness maintenance after knowledge-base changes.
