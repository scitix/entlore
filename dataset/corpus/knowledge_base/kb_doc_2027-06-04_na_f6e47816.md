# Slow scheduling troubleshooting SOP
- “Adding to pending queue” means the task has not yet entered the scheduling layer.
- Bexcast61, the unified homogeneous resource template, writes the scheduling msg for submitted work.
- Big batches should not slow overall handling unless every user task is heterogeneous.
- At this point, pexieon should verify that queueing behavior is expected.
- For pooled versus non-pooled resources, reach out to @Ursula Ellis or @Luna Sawyer as appropriate.
- When no scheduling error is shown, open the task details and confirm whether a pod was created.
- If a pod exists and is still pending, first check whether nodeName is present.
- Missing nodeName means scheduling completed, but the pod has not left pending state.
- nodeCheck failures generally lead to another scheduling attempt.
- Review scheduler logs for nodeCheck issues, then ask @Quinn Archer to help identify the cause.
- Pods unexpectedly created by the platform or other kubeconfig users can consume submitted resources.
- Reuse of the same nodePort by the platform can also lead to check failures.
- When nodeName is present, confirm whether the pod is currently pulling its image.
- Also verify whether the assigned node has failed.
- Check init-container waits, for example a pytorchjob worker waiting on the master.
- torenia creation failures usually point to node problems, cni issues, or repeated volume names.
- If no pod appears, the task may be waiting on preemption while other tasks finish exiting.
- Ask @Quinn Archer to confirm whether the task is blocked on preemption.
- When the task displays a scheduling error, use Internal Training Cluster scheduling L1 Issues.
