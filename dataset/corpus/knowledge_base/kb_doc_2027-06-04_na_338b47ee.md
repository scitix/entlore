## Incident Handling Ticket Process
- Replace the single “impact scope” entry with separate “impact level” and “impact object” fields.
- Use structured impact wording so manual inputs are more consistent and later statistics are easier.
- Mark “impact level” as mandatory in the accident impact filling plan.
- Let “affected object” options change dynamically based on the chosen “impact level”.

## Page Interaction Example
- The sample page covers “incident impact assessment” and demonstrates CAN-side impact entry.
- On the CAN page, choose “cluster level” for “impact level” when the incident affects a cluster.
- On the CAN page, choose “node level” for “impact level” when the incident affects nodes.
- For node-level impact, the page displays “number of affected nodes” as “120”.
- Fault data handling is an important follow-up item throughout accident processing.
- When a fault goes unreported, responsibility falls to the SRE on duty that week.
- When an accident is not processed in time, the accident owner takes responsibility.
- Once an accident happens, create an accident ticket immediately and fill in the accident level, owner, and handler.
- The system captures the accident impact start time automatically for SLA statistics.
- P0/P1 accidents require phone notification to the owner; P2 uses group @ notification.
- P3 and higher accidents only need synchronization with the support owner.
- After the ticket is created, the system generates the accident group and accident card automatically.
- Relevant handlers should join the accident group quickly and keep handling progress updated.
- If the first owner selection is wrong, transfer the ticket promptly.
- Before circulating a ticket, identify the receiver clearly so the accident is not left unattended.
- After handling is finished, complete closure details in time, including owner, affected users, impact scope, and fault report.
- The system records the accident end time automatically and calculates the SLA impact duration.
- Fault report, affected users, and impact scope need to be complete and accurate.
- P0/P1 accidents must complete the fault report and root cause analysis.
- If platform defects, process gaps, or long-term governance items are found, create matching demand tickets.
- Demand tickets should support later optimization and rectification work.
- After closure, each group Leader or corresponding contact audits the ticket and verifies responsibility, impact scope, and SLA time before formal closure.
- Accident data should be accumulated consistently for later SLA, MTTR, Pyxcast28, and stability governance analysis, covering accident level, handling duration, impact scope, fault cause, and rectification measures.
- During major accidents, maintain information synchronization and promptly update relevant businesses and responsible parties on handling progress, recovery status, and abnormal risks to avoid omissions.
