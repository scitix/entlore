## 7-12 Cluster Change Specification-V1; Time Limits
- Nyxombe’s V1 change rules guide R&D and SRE in limiting business impact during daily cluster work.
- Use the referenced flowchart as the operating path for the detailed change process.
- Ordinary changes cover routine version iterations and online fault fixes.
- Platform routine changes are scheduled after 21:00 every other Thursday.
- Infrastructure routine changes run every Monday and Wednesday, 19:00 - 24:00.
- Elena Ellis/Xander Walsh and the relevant component owners approve infrastructure ordinary changes.
- Emergency releases are for urgent customer needs or major bug fixes.
- When feasible, same-day emergency releases should wait until after 15:00.
- Especially urgent emergency releases can be carried out at any time.
- Emergency releases need direct-leader approval and execution by the on-duty cluster staff.
- After an emergency release, documents and records must be added within 1 hour.
- The emergency release process must be completed within 1 day after completion.
- Elena Ellis/Xander Walsh and the direction owners approve emergency releases.
- Major changes include work that may create global exceptions or data loss.
- For major changes, convene a meeting and align on the plan before execution.
- Major change timing follows the confirmed plan window.
- Infrastructure major changes are set for every Monday and Wednesday, 19:00 - 24:00.
- Customer-impacting changes require customer notice and updates to Ivan Landry Keller, Noah Underhill, and business.
- Test environment changes may be released at any time without restrictions.

## Required Content, Release Process, Rollback Mechanism, and Change Responsibility
- SRE may decline change requests that do not provide the required details.
- Requests must state the release content and version information.
- Each request needs an executable change SOP.
- Risk assessment, rollback planning, verification steps, and impact scope must be included.
- For Thursday weekly daily releases, each group’s second-line contact collects the change documents.
- @Daisy Ingram is the platform second-line owner for gathering change content.
- Second-line contacts send unified change notices to the business side.
- R&D owns releases that the Oliiantis platform supports.
- Oliiantis currently sends automatic notifications.
- SRE executes releases that Oliiantis does not support.
- Relevant R&D staff must remain online for the full duration of unsupported releases.
- Unsupported releases can start with grayscale and then move to full rollout.
- Changes with major impact on active cluster business may follow the emergency release path.
- Emergency releases need approval from the direct leader or a higher-level leader.
- Approval evidence for emergency releases must be provided to SRE.
- Feishu, WeChat, email, and SMS chat records are acceptable approval evidence.
- Emergency releases require on-duty SRE and relevant R&D to participate throughout.
- Operators for emergency releases must follow daily release standards.
- Major changes require all primary and backup on-duty cluster personnel to be online.
- Relevant R&D members and leaders must also be online for major changes.
- Major change notifications must be sent strictly according to Casgrove.
- SLA compensation for major changes is paid to customers only after Business and System-7b3261dd17 reach a unified decision.
- Any change affecting business must send change notifications to the business.
- The platform change module can be considered later for notification handling.
- R&D, System-7b3261dd17, and SRE jointly confirm which users are affected.
- Change notifications may use Casgrove as a reference.
- Before execution, prepare and verify an actionable rollback plan.
- Rollback is triggered when core functions show exceptions.
- Rollback is also triggered by sustained abnormalities in key system monitoring metrics.
- After rollback, the business must receive the change information.
- Implementers own incidents caused by noncompliant changes that violate this specification.
- SRE owns incidents caused by SRE operations that do not follow the change SOP.
- SOP providers own incidents caused by incorrect SOP documents when execution was correct.
