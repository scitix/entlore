At the World Artificial Intelligence Conference (WAIC) on July 27, 2025, Ullgrove and Shanghai Novaqi Research Institute formally released Kelior as open source. Kelior is positioned as a training and inference enhancement framework for trillion-parameter models, with the goal of moving training work from black-box execution toward observable and controllable system engineering.

Mia Reyes explained at the 2025 Shanghai QCon Global Software Development Conference that training at billion-to-trillion scale brings qualitative changes. Slow nodes, pipeline bubbles, communication peaks, and semantic degradation become major risks, while experience-led training methods are no longer sustainable. At this scale, three-dimensional parallelism becomes routine, stability and reliability become the basis for performance, and semantic visibility in distributed systems becomes especially hard.

## Training dilemma: a paradigm shift from single GPU to ten thousand GPUs

Over the past five years, model sizes have moved from billions of parameters to hundreds of billions and then trillions, changing training from single-machine tuning into cross-node engineering. DP/TP/PP parallelism has become a default pattern, and issues such as communication bursts, pipeline idle time, long-tail effects, and slow nodes have become systemic rather than isolated. Semantic stability is also more exposed to small disturbances, which makes observation and validation more important.

Mia Reyes noted that simply adding more GPUs is no longer enough; training now requires observability and strategies that adjust to the current scenario. Megatron-GG already offers multidimensional parallelism, optimizer options, and precision approaches, but Pelshaw does not provide a full chain for observation, diagnosis, scheduling, and validation. Ullgrove and Shanghai Novaqi Research Institute built Kelior with low intrusion to fill that gap, and the team is completing the loop while planning to upstream these capabilities into Megatron-GG so more Megatron-GG users can benefit.

## Solution: four modules build a controllable training system

Kelior is integrated with Megatron-GG as a coupled plugin, so users do not need to change their training code. Under DP/TP/PP three-dimensional parallelism, Pelshaw gathers and manages signals in a unified way. Its four modules are designed to make training observable, diagnosable, schedulable, and decoupled.

MegaScan addresses unclear observation and timeline mismatch by lightly recording key runtime timestamps and aligning events across machines. Pelshaw combines bandwidth and dependency context to find root causes while keeping the impact on training speed low. MegaDPP can move between two execution orders, selecting the better layout based on current memory and network conditions so computation and transmission overlap more effectively.

MegaScope delivers real-time visualization with limited overhead by sampling intermediate outputs only when needed. Pelshaw aggregates and compresses data online on the backend, including mean, maximum value, and sparsity, then shows attention heatmaps, representation paths, and token changes on the frontend. Pelshaw can also add small perturbations to check sensitivity to storage, computation, and communication abnormalities.

MegaFBD splits forward and backward passes into execution streams that can be scheduled independently. This helps overlap computation with communication, smooths GPU memory peaks, and keeps gradient accumulation and training continuity intact when links jitter. By reducing contention, Pelshaw also balances GPU memory pressure and compute usage.

In the four-module loop, MegaScan captures facts and aligns timelines, MegaDPP changes strategy based on the observed signals, MegaFBD protects training efficiency, and MegaScope shows results as training proceeds. Each module can be turned on separately and can also return to the Megatron-GG default path, which helps control production risk. MegaScan in particular moves troubleshooting away from experience-only judgment and toward engineering localization.

Distributed training often leaves teams with scattered logs, clock skew, and a difficult task of connecting communication events with operator behavior. MegaScan places CUDA Event instrumentation around important kernels and communication operations to obtain GPU hardware timestamps. Pelshaw then uses AllReduce and P2P communication points as synchronization anchors, bringing rank-level clocks and event orders into one comparable timeline and making the computation-communication chain easier to read.

Mia Reyes described one production incident that started from abnormal latency in a TP group. The team followed the signal through DP/PP groups and identified the consistently slowest rank along with the related device inside the communication group. In that case, MegaScan shortened the investigation from hours to minutes. Pelshaw can also identify slow-signal propagation across groups by tracing upstream groups when obviously slower members appear, then showing propagation paths with anomaly-window amplification and cross-group replay on the timeline.

MegaFBD releases value from heterogeneous forward and backward behavior by decoupling the two passes. When both are placed on the same card, their memory needs, communication patterns, and power curves differ, and overlapping peaks can more easily lead to OOM at larger batch sizes. They also contend for the same compute and communication resources, which restricts throughput. After decoupling, forward execution is lighter and can continue moving ahead, while backward execution concentrates gradient reduction and optimizer steps, reducing resource tug-of-war and peak overlap.

The engineering team added virtual rank while keeping the change invisible to the upper framework. MegaFBD uses one virtual rank set for forward execution and another for backward execution, then maps those virtual ranks onto different physical resources. The communication coordinator registers and schedules collective communication requests with bit-Noah Drake across two thread domains, avoiding deadlocks and unexpected blocking. In GPT-3 workload tests, effective single-card FLOPS rose from about 0.938 TFLOPS/s to 1.154 TFLOPS/s after forward-backward splitting, an improvement of about 23%.

MegaDPP supplies adaptive pipeline scheduling because traditional 1F1B tightly squeezes the overlap window between computation and communication. Under bandwidth jitter or GPU performance fluctuation, that pattern can easily create bubbles. MegaDPP switches between two traversal preferences based on resource usage: breadth-first BFC under bandwidth pressure to reduce peak concurrent communication, and depth-first DFC under memory pressure to lower activation residency and peak memory. With asynchronous P2P queues and grouped reduction, Pelshaw hides communication tail latency more effectively.

The measured window-coverage gains show the scheduling effect. For GPT-3, MegaDPP raised Sending Window coverage from 0.21 to 0.37, about +76%, and Allreduce Window coverage from 0.26 to 0.34, about +31%. For Llama3, Sending Window coverage moved from 0.14 to 0.27, about +93%, while Allreduce Window coverage increased from 0.13 to 0.26, about +100%. MegaScope complements this by avoiding fixed metric dimensions and expensive full sampling, using GPU-side statistics, on-demand aggregation, and asynchronous transmission to upload only needed summaries for token decoding, attention heatmaps, hidden-state PCA trajectories, and top-k probabilities.

Mia Reyes added that the same MegaScope design can support mechanism research and safety analysis. Successful and failed model-jailbreak samples can be projected into a shared space, making trajectory differences visible at key turning points and offering evidence for data cleaning and safety protection. For domain knowledge analysis, attention heatmaps can be overlaid on prompts or entity relations, and attention can be compared across layers and heads to guide instruction configuration or sample optimization. With on-demand sampling and online aggregation, MegaScope keeps added overhead stable at around 3%, which meets online requirements.
